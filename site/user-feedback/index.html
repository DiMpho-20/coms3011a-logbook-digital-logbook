    User Feedback

    This page documents the formal user feedback process we use to improve the Digital Logbook. Feedback is collected through a standardised instrument — the System Usability Scale (SUS) — combined with task-scenario observations and open-ended responses. Findings are tracked and every actionable change is recorded in the integration table at the bottom of this page.

    Why we collect feedback

    Automated tests verify that the code does what we intend; user feedback verifies that the product works for the people it's for. The feedback process is a required part of our methodology: every round of collection is followed by a round of integration, where each actionable finding becomes a tracked issue with an assigned fix.

    The formal process

    Stage What happens
    Instrument A Google Form with the 10 standard SUS items, round-specific self-report questions, and an open-ended prompt.
    Testers 5–8 per round: classmates, members of other teams, and our client (where available). Demographics are captured in the form itself.
    Session format 15–20 minutes, supervised. The tester works through the 7 task scenarios on the live app while an observer notes pass/fail and friction points on the observation sheet below.
    Recording Form responses land in a linked Google Sheet. An observer's written notes are kept alongside as a second data source.
    Analysis SUS scores per tester (0–100) and an average across the round; per-task pass/fail counts; open-ended responses grouped by theme.
    Integration Every actionable finding becomes an issue on the bug tracker with an owner and a fix commit. The integration table below records the full trail.

    The feedback form

    Round 1 form: https://forms.gle/g4gEzYtiFRSXCQ5CA

    Round 2 form (Sprint 3 revision): https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSenlkuuQC3bUS3WPXpBDO8M0O4eNDGurk669OKzzlunm0X_ow/viewform

    The Round 1 form is structured in four sections:

    1. Welcome & About You — consent statement, role (Student / Lecturer or tutor / Working professional / Other), and prior experience with digital logbooks or work-tracking tools.
    2. Task Feedback — for each of 7 tasks (register, create project, add entry, mark done, view stats, view calendar/board, enable 2FA), the tester self-reports Completed easily / Completed with some difficulty / Could not complete, plus an optional comment field per task.
    3. System Usability Scale — the 10 standard SUS items on a 5-point Likert scale (Strongly disagree → Strongly agree). Worded verbatim; the SUS instrument is free to use and publicly cited across HCI research.
    4. Open Feedback — most frustrating part (required), favourite feature, one-thing-to-change, and an NPS-style 0–10 recommend-likelihood scale.

    For Round 2 the questionnaire was revised so that every question maps to a criterion in the Sprint 3 rubric: the SUS instrument was kept intact (one 10-statement grid), and the task self-reports were replaced with feature-usage, save-correctness, load-speed, entry-field clarity, Calendar/Board discoverability, visual-integrity and one-thing-to-change questions.

    A screenshot of the published form is captured after each round. The raw response data for each round is stored as a CSV file in this repository (docs/user-feedback-round1.csv and docs/user-feedback-round2.csv).

    Observation sheet (for the supervisor)

    Self-reported answers are one half of the evidence; the other half is what the observer actually sees. For each session, fill in this table on paper alongside the tester:

    Task Completed Notes / friction points
    1. Register a new account ✅ / ⚠️ / ❌
    2. Log in and create a project with custom fields ✅ / ⚠️ / ❌
    3. Add a logbook entry (with duration + due date) ✅ / ⚠️ / ❌
    4. Mark an entry as done ✅ / ⚠️ / ❌
    5. Check project statistics (totals, averages) ✅ / ⚠️ / ❌
    6. Find calendar and board views ✅ / ⚠️ / ❌
    7. Enable two-factor authentication ✅ / ⚠️ / ❌

    ✅ = completed easily · ⚠️ = completed with difficulty · ❌ = could not complete.

    SUS scoring (in the linked Google Sheet)

    When the form is linked to a Google Sheet (Responses → create spreadsheet), add these helper columns to auto-score each row:

    Column Header Formula
    SUS_1 q1_adj =B2-1 (odd item: subtract 1)
    SUS_2 q2_adj =5-C2 (even item: 5 − answer)
    SUS_3 q3_adj =D2-1
    SUS_4 q4_adj =5-E2
    SUS_5 q5_adj =F2-1
    SUS_6 q6_adj =5-G2
    SUS_7 q7_adj =H2-1
    SUS_8 q8_adj =5-I2
    SUS_9 q9_adj =J2-1
    SUS_10 q10_adj =5-K2
    SUS score =SUM(q1_adj:q10_adj) * 2.5 (yields 0–100)

    Adjust column letters to match the actual sheet columns produced by the grid question. The pattern — odd items score answer − 1, even items score 5 − answer, then sum and multiply by 2.5 — is the canonical SUS calculation from Brooke (1996).

    Average SUS across the round: =AVERAGE(SUS_score_range). Industry benchmarks (Sauro, 2011) place the average SUS at 68/100; anything above ~72 is above-average, above ~80 is excellent.

    One small change four "nothing"; one "nothing besides faster login"; one "year-to-date calculator / grade tracking"

    Findings

    • Login latency is the one recurring friction. Three testers named login or registration as the slowest moment, and nobody reported any other performance problem — consistent with the free hosting tier's cold start (see the integration table for what was done about it).
    • Reliability held up. Five of six testers saw no misbehaviour and reported that saving worked every time; the two exceptions were single occurrences with no reproduction detail.
    • Round 1's friction points did not resurface. No tester was confused by entry fields, and every tester found the Calendar and Board views straight away. The queued FEAT-002 / FEAT-003 improvements remain in the integration table.

    Summary and Next Steps

    Round 1 (SUS 72.9) and Round 2 (SUS 72.5; 77.0 excluding one straight-line response) both sit above the industry average of 68, and Round 2 held up on reliability: five of six testers saw no misbehaviour and reported that saving worked every time. The one recurring complaint across rounds is login/registration latency, which matches the free hosting tier's cold start.

    Round 1's friction points (entry-field clarity, view discoverability) did not resurface in Round 2; FEAT-002 and FEAT-003 have been partly addressed, as the integration table records.

    Planned actions:

    1. Keep the API awake during the day with the scheduled pinger (done); moving off the free tier would remove the night-time cold start too.
    2. Finish FEAT-002 (field-type badges, formula tooltips) and FEAT-003 (a first-visit tour).
    3. Run Round 3, including a client review session, and fill in the "Validated in" column.

    (Done since Round 2: the grade-tracking suggestion was built as My Grades — see the integration table above.)
